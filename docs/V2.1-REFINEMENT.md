## VB-73 ✅ · The splash becomes a configuration gate
Today it plays once per session and any input skips it. Change: **it stays up until a click**, and
carries the decisions someone should make before reading the first instruction.

- **"Load Your File"** → a loading walkthrough.
- **"Build Your File"** → Home, with a CTA to build Context first.
- **Toggles for audio and microphone**, so the app is configured before anything starts.

**⚠️ The microphone toggle conflicts with a settled decision.** V1.8's VB-49 established, with four
independent blockers, that **this product does not build a microphone** — audio leaves the device by
default and falls back to cloud *silently*, the permission prompt cannot render in a side panel at
all, `audioCapture` is gated to a Google allowlist, and cloud recognition is disclosable collection
against a listing that declares none. Nothing has changed. So decide what the toggle actually is:
- **(a)** it controls the OS-dictation *hint* (whether we mention Fn/Win+H at all) — honest, cheap,
  but a strange thing to promote to a splash decision; or
- **(b)** it is dropped, and the splash offers audio only.
**(b) is the honest option.** A toggle labelled "microphone" that turns on a hint rather than a
microphone is the kind of small dishonesty this product has avoided everywhere else.

**Built as (b), stated plainly rather than silently:** the splash offers the two doors and the
audio row, no microphone anything. Also built: "Load your file" attempts to open the import picker
directly (silent fallback: the person lands beside Home's "I already have a file"), the covered
panel is `inert` while the splash shows, and once-per-session arrival is unchanged. One copy note
for review: **"Load your file" is a literal substring of Home's "Download your file"** — found
because a role query matched both — and the two are opposite moves wearing near-identical names.

Note the splash also stops being skippable-by-anything, which was VB-34's protection against it
becoming a toll booth. Staying up until a *click* is fine — it now has a decision on it, so it is a
doorway rather than an interstitial. But it must still never gate the first paint.

## VB-74 ✅ · The visual's nav is fixed, not floating
Lock the back and centre controls to **a nav row below the breadcrumbs**. They must **not travel
with the visual**.

Two levels, and both persist through every click-through into nodes and details, so the way out is
always the same shape:
- **Back** — one level up.
- **Home** — all the way out to the Context / Skills / Actions grouping. The high-level equivalent
  of the Home screen.

This supersedes VB-59's floating disc and VB-71's corner placement — both were positioned *inside*
the stage. Moving them out is also what makes VB-75 possible, since the stage stops needing to
reserve corner space.

## VB-75 ✅ · The visual stays wide, or it isn't shown
Keep the visual's frame **wide** as the panel is resized. Below a threshold, **switch to list by
default** — do not render a smaller globe.

Adam's reasoning, and it should be quoted in the code: *"It either is important enough to see at
reading size or not important enough to use and just stay in list view."*

**This tightens VB-70 rather than replacing it.** VB-70 already pins the threshold to
`brainStageRoom` vs `brainStageSize` — the point where the globe stops shrinking and starts
clipping. VB-75 says the globe should stop *shrinking* much earlier: there is a minimum legible
size, and below it the answer is List, not a smaller picture. So the threshold moves up, and the
"scale to fit" behaviour between the two sizes goes away.

Keep VB-70's hysteresis and its "Brain becomes available again, not Brain reopens" rule.

## VB-77 ✅ · Orb labels stop blinking
Labels become **fixed relative to their orb** and **scale with it**, staying proportionate through
the rotation. The blink in and out is distracting and buys nothing.

**This reverses a decision made deliberately**, so it should be made knowingly: VB-14/VB-31 hid
labels during motion because *"text tumbling in 3D is unreadable and slightly nauseating"* — and
Adam's own original note said motion should carry shape while rest carries information. What is
being asked for instead is labels that ride their orb rather than the camera, which is a different
thing and avoids the tumbling problem: the label never rotates, it only moves and scales.

Adam has already acknowledged the cost: rendering text that tracks a projected 3D position each
frame is heavier than fading it. Expect real work on legibility — a far orb's label will be small,
and `docs/GUARDRAILS.md`'s floors do not scale down with it. **Set a minimum readable size and
decide what happens when an orb is too far to carry a legible label** (drop the label, or clamp the
size and accept overlap). That decision is the task.

## VB-78 · The escape hatch — SPIKED, not built (docs/SPIKE-escape-hatch.md)
Today's flow lets someone *iterate or improve* an answer they have already written. It offers
nothing for the harder moment: a blank field on a question that feels consequential and costly to
get wrong.

Make the pre-seeded prompt a **live button** on those questions — a way to get a first pass out of
their own AI and bring it back, before they have written anything.

**Most of the machinery exists.** `interpret.buildPrompt` already composes a real prompt per
question (six questions carry one), the proof loop already runs the whole copy-out/paste-back
mechanic, and the reflect step already accepts a pasted result as the answer. What is missing is
the *entry point before there is any text to improve*.

**The tone Adam is aiming at, in his words:** *"These questions need to feel like a therapist
helping you unlock the pain points in your work life… keep the bar to entry low and the ability to
thrive on the other side of the bar high. Under promise. Over deliver."*

Worth iterating on rather than building blind — a button labelled "write this for me" invites a
file full of someone else's words, which is the one outcome that would hollow out the product. The
version that works probably asks the AI to *interview* them rather than answer for them, or returns
options to react to rather than prose to accept. **Spike this before building it.**

## Answered — the dictation hint (Adam's question)
*"Does pressing the Fn key twice work as a standard, or is it providing the instruction because it
is detecting Wispr Flow?"*

**Neither — it detects nothing.** `src/core/flow/dictation.ts` says so in its own header: *"Nothing
in this module reads a device, a permission or a capability. It takes the user-agent string it is
given and answers two questions."* It sees "this is a Mac" and prints the macOS default.

**And that copy is less true than it sounds — worth fixing.** `S.dictationMac` states the shortcut
as fact: *"Press the Fn key twice, then talk."* In reality:
- Dictation is **off by default** on a fresh Mac and must be enabled in System Settings first.
- The shortcut is **user-configurable** and can be changed or disabled.
- **Third-party tools commonly bind the same double-tap** — Wispr Flow among them. If Adam has it
  installed, Fn-Fn is very likely launching Wispr rather than macOS Dictation, which is exactly why
  the question arose.

So the hint can be wrong in three ordinary situations, and it currently asserts rather than
suggests. **Fix the copy to point at the capability rather than the keystroke** — something that
survives all three cases — and keep it inside the reading-level check.
