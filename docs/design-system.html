The design system

A light-first system built to feel immediately familiar to anyone who has used a modern business application — and to stay legible to someone who does not think of themselves as technical. Every value here is checked against WCAG 2.1 AA, and every rule exists because breaking it costs comprehension, not because it looks nicer.

01Foundations

Five rules. If a screen breaks one of them, the screen is wrong — not the rule.

1 · One decision per screen

Never two primary buttons. Never a question inside a question. The person should be able to say out loud what this screen is asking before they act.

2 · The file is always visible

Nothing accumulates in hidden state. If an answer changed the file, the file shows it immediately. Trust comes from watching the thing get built.

3 · Color names who owns the next move

Blue: Workbrain is asking. Amber: time is asking. Violet: this belongs to your AI. Green: done. Grey: your turn to type. No decorative color anywhere.

4 · Motion is instruction

An animation says do this next, and stops the instant it is done. Nothing loops for atmosphere. Every cue has a still-image equivalent for reduced motion.

5 · Never a dead end

Every screen has a way forward that does not require understanding the screen. Skip is always available. Nothing is ever unrecoverable, so nothing needs a confirmation dialog.

The audience assumption

Design for a competent adult who has never installed a browser extension, is slightly worried they will break something, and will not read anything longer than two lines. That person is the median user, not the edge case.

Dark mode is a preference, not the identity. It ships, it inverts the neutrals, and it keeps the same four meanings at their dark-surface equivalents. It is not where design decisions get made, and no screen is ever designed dark-first — that is how contrast bugs get shipped to the majority of users.

Spacing

A 4px base, used in multiples of 4 and 8. Component padding comes from a fixed set so that two cards built by two people line up: 8 · 12 · 16 · 20 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64.

Density is comfortable, never compact. A 400px panel is tempting to cram. Resist it — a screen that fits one more field is a screen that reads as a form, and forms are where non-technical users stop.

Flat surfaces carry a 1px border, not a shadow. Shadow means this is on top of something, and is reserved for exactly that. Three levels, no more.

04Components

Nine components carry the entire product. Each is documented with its variants, its states, and what it is not allowed to do.

Button

Primary · Secondary · AI action · Quiet · Disabled · Small. Minimum height 44px in every variant.
VariantUse whenRule
PrimaryThe one thing this screen wantsExactly one per screen. If you need two, the screen is asking two questions.
SecondaryA real alternative, equally validBordered, never filled. Two maximum.
AI actionThe action hands off to the person's own AIViolet. Always paired with one line saying exactly what it will do.
QuietSkip, cancel, not nowNever disabled, never hidden. Escape hatches must always be available.

States: default → hover (background shift, no movement) → focus-visible (2px blue ring, 2px offset) → active (no transform) → disabled (40% opacity, never removed from the DOM) → loading (label changes to a verb in progress, e.g. working… — no spinner-only states, a spinner alone tells a nervous user nothing).

Choice pill

Selection is carried by fill, weight and a checkmark — three signals, so color is never the only one.

Pills replace text entry wherever a reasonable set of answers exists. This is the single highest-leverage decision for low-confidence users: recognition beats recall, and a blank box is where people quit. Every pill row ends with + add your own, so the list never becomes a cage.

Behaviour: single-select rows commit on click and advance nothing — the person still presses Next, because auto-advance removes their sense of control. Multi-select rows toggle. Arrow keys move between pills, Space selects, and the group is a role="group" with an accessible name from the question.

Note what is missing from the home screen. No navigation bar, no tabs, no settings page, no search. A person opens the panel and sees a number, a reason, and one button. Every additional entry point is a decision the median user has to make before they can start, and each one measurably reduces the number who do.

06Motion

Four cues, each meaning exactly one instruction, each stopping the moment it is followed. This is the system's one genuinely distinctive idea, and it survives the move to light mode unchanged.

Sweep — pick from this row
Ring — press this
Copy this prompt
Violet ring — this is your AI's
Bob — take this with you
Each cue is declared in data on the step, not written in CSS per screen.
DurationUse
120msHover, focus, color change. Below perception — the UI feels solid, not sluggish.
200msScreen transitions, content entering. The default for anything that moves.
320msSheets, drawers, anything covering something else.
cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)The only easing curve. Fast out, settled in.

Reduced motion is not "animations off." Every cue has a still equivalent that carries the same instruction — the sweep becomes a steady tint, the ring becomes a fixed halo, the bob stops but the button keeps its emphasis. Turning off motion must never remove guidance, because the people most likely to enable it are often the people who most need the guidance.

07Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor, and it is not a compliance exercise here — the same decisions that satisfy it are the decisions that make the product usable by someone who is nervous about technology.

CriterionHow this system meets it
1.4.3 Contrast (AA)All text ≥ 4.5:1; measured values published in section 02. No text on a hue below 4.5 anywhere in the product.
1.4.11 Non-text contrastEvery interactive boundary uses --border-i at 3.13:1. Focus rings at 5.6:1.
1.4.1 Use of colorSelection = fill + weight + checkmark. Staleness = color + badge text + a sentence. Nothing is color-only.
1.4.4 Resize textPanel layout holds at 200% zoom; type scale in rem; no fixed-height text containers.
2.1.1 KeyboardComplete flow keyboard-only: Tab between regions, arrows within pill groups, Space to select, Enter for primary.
2.4.7 Focus visible2px blue ring, 2px offset, on every focusable element. Never suppressed.
2.5.5 Target size44 × 44 minimum for every control, including icon buttons and pills.
2.2.1 TimingNothing expires. Toasts auto-dismiss but never carry the only copy of anything.
2.3.3 Animation from interactionsprefers-reduced-motion respected on every cue, with instruction preserved.
3.3.1 / 3.3.3 ErrorsErrors describe the fix, are tied with aria-describedby, and never use color alone.
4.1.3 Status messagesToasts and freshness changes announce via aria-live="polite"; nothing steals focus.

Beyond the standard

Read aloud is a feature, not a novelty

The narrator is the accessibility story for people who read slowly, read in a second language, or are tired. It reads questions and hand-off instructions — never the person's own answers back at them in a room where someone might hear.

Speak your answers

Dictation removes the blank-box problem entirely for people who type slowly. It is offered before question one and stays togglable, and it never becomes the only way to answer anything.

No timed anything

No session expiry, no auto-advance, no "you've been idle." The interview can take three weeks of two-minute sittings and lose nothing.

One reading level

Target grade 7. Measured, not assumed — every string in the product gets checked before it ships, the same way every color does.

08Words

The interface is mostly words, so the words are part of the design system. These are binding, not stylistic.

The rules

RuleWhy
Second person, present tense, active voice"Pick the AI you use most," not "An AI should be selected."
No product nouns the person did not bringThey know "file," "notes," "list." They do not know "context object," "artifact," "schema," "instance."
Numbers as words where small"Two questions" reads as easy. "2 questions" reads as a form.
Sentences under 20 words, one idea eachThe panel is 400px wide. Long sentences become walls.
Buttons are verbs the person would say"Answer 2 questions," not "Continue." "Take my file," not "Export."
Never apologise, never blame"That didn't save — try again" beats both "Oops!" and "Invalid input."
Say what happens next, every timeEspecially before anything touches another tab.
