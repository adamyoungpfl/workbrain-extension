# V1.1 copy — first draft, for review

Everything new that `docs/V1.1-REFINEMENT.md` depends on. **Nothing here is built yet** — this is the
draft to edit before any of it gets wired up.

Written against `docs/design-system.html` §08 (binding): second person, present tense, active voice;
no product nouns the person didn't bring; sentences under 20 words; small numbers as words; buttons
are verbs they'd say; never apologise, never blame. Target reading grade 7 — `npm run audit` measures
this, so edits should stay in the same register.

Two standing constraints this copy respects, both from `docs/GUARDRAILS.md`:
- **No tour, no coach-marks.** These screens are in-flow narrative — the person is reading the thing
  they're already doing, not being pointed at UI by a dismissible overlay.
- **No nudges framed as guilt.** Your "biggest risk is failure to launch" line is in here, but
  reframed to be about momentum rather than fault. Both versions are shown at VB-06 so you can pick.

## VB-03 · Deeper-dive questions

`Step.deepDive` entries. **Decided: every hinted question gets one** — 24 questions carrying a hint
today, plus `context_scope` and `rigor_before_acting`, which have no hint but clearly want one. 26
in total.

**One exception I'd argue for, and am flagging rather than quietly applying.** Three questions —
`voice_directness`, `voice_formality`, `voice_qualification` — carry hints that are *worked
examples*, not explanations:

> Diplomatic: "There's a chance Friday could be tight, given how the vendor work has gone."
> Balanced: "Friday is at risk — the vendor delay pushed us back." Blunt: "This won't be ready Friday."

Those samples are what make the question answerable at a glance. Hiding them behind a tap makes the
question *harder*, not easier — the opposite of what this feature is for. So for those three the
examples stay visible inline, and the deep-dive adds something the hint doesn't already say. Every
other hinted question converts as asked. Overrule me on this if you'd rather it be uniform.

### `orientation_ready` — "We're going to build a Context file..."
Your example. The existing hint becomes the answer.

**Q:** What is a Context file?
**A:** A short, plain-text file — a few pages, not a database. You answer questions here, and the
file gets written for you. You download it and hand it to whatever AI you use.

**Q:** Why would AI need this?
**A:** AI starts every conversation knowing nothing about you. This is the part you'd otherwise
retype every time.

### `context_scope` — "work, personal life, or both?"
**Q:** What changes based on my answer?
**A:** Only the wording of later questions. Pick "both" if you're unsure — nothing gets locked out.

### `role_names` — "What are your roles...?"
**Q:** What counts as a role?
**A:** Anything you'd describe differently to different people. A job is a role. So is running a
fundraiser, or coaching a team.

**Q:** What if I only have one?
**A:** That's normal — most people have one or two. Pick one and keep going.

### `responsibilities_list` — "What are you personally responsible for?"
**Q:** How is this different from what I do all day?
**A:** Responsibility is what lands on you if it goes wrong. You do plenty of things you're not
ultimately on the hook for.

### `contribution_boundaries` — "What work do you contribute to but do not own?"
**Q:** Why does AI need to know what I don't own?
**A:** So it stops writing as if a call is yours to make. It changes how things get worded.

### `decision_rights` — "What kinds of decisions can you make yourself?"
**Q:** What if it depends?
**A:** It usually does. Say what you can decide alone, and where the line is. "Under ten thousand,
my call" is a real answer.

### `entities_gate` — "Are there specific people, teams, tools...?"
**Q:** What kind of thing belongs here?
**A:** Anything you name constantly and then have to explain. A manager, a teammate, a tool you
live in, a meeting that matters.

**Q:** Should I add my whole team?
**A:** No. Only the ones AI would need to recognize to be useful. Two or three is plenty.

### `initiatives_gate` — "Are there specific initiatives or projects...?"
**Q:** What makes something an initiative?
**A:** It has a name, a goal, and an end. Your ongoing responsibilities aren't initiatives — those
came up earlier.

### `risk_tolerance` — "fast, good-enough call or slower, more certain one?"
**Q:** What if it depends on the situation?
**A:** Pick "depends on the stakes" — it's a real answer, not a cop-out. AI will ask when it
matters.

### `rigor_before_acting` — "How much reasoning do you want to see...?"
**Q:** What does this actually change?
**A:** Whether AI hands you a two-line answer or walks you through how it got there. You can always
ask for more in the moment.

### `guardrails_list` — "What should AI never do without asking you first?"
**Q:** What happens if I leave these on?
**A:** AI drafts instead of sending, and asks before changing anything. Most people keep all three
on.

**Q:** Can I change these later?
**A:** Yes. Everything here can be answered again — nothing is locked in.

### `negative_responsibility` — "What do people assume is yours but isn't?"
**Q:** Why would AI need to know this?
**A:** So it stops writing as if you can approve things you can't. It's the most common way drafts
come out wrong.

**Q:** What if nothing comes to mind?
**A:** Skip it. This one's genuinely optional.

### `contribution_boundaries` — "What do you contribute to but not own?"
**Q:** Should I say who does own it?
**A:** If you know, yes. "The product lead decides" tells AI more than "not mine."

### `role_for` — "Who or what is this role for?"
**Q:** What if it's for more than one?
**A:** Pick the one you'd name first if someone asked. This just anchors who the role serves — it
isn't a contract.

### `expertise` — "What expertise do people normally come to you for?"
**Q:** What if I don't feel like an expert?
**A:** You don't have to be the best at it. If people ask you first, that counts.

**Q:** Does depth matter here?
**A:** Yes, and it's worth saying. Deep expertise and working knowledge get treated differently —
tell AI which is which.

### `entity_aliases` — "Any other names, nicknames, or shorthand?"
**Q:** Why does this matter?
**A:** So AI recognizes the thing when you use its short name. If you say "the CRM" and it only
knows "Salesforce," you're explaining it again.

**Q:** What if there's only one name?
**A:** Then skip it. Most things only have one.

### `autonomy_preference` — "When AI can clearly see the next step..."
**Q:** Isn't this the same as the last question?
**A:** Close, but no. That one was about unclear requests. This one is about clear ones — whether
AI should move or check with you first.

**Q:** What if it depends on how risky the step is?
**A:** A later question covers the hard rules — what AI must never do without asking. This one is
just your general instinct.

### `voice_directness` — "How direct should AI default to...?"
*The three worked examples stay visible inline — see the note above.*

**Q:** Is this locked in?
**A:** No. It's the starting point. You can always ask for something softer or blunter in the
moment.

### `voice_formality` — "How formal should the default tone be?"
*Examples stay visible inline.*

**Q:** What if it depends who's reading?
**A:** It usually does — there's a whole section on that later. Pick your most common case here.

### `voice_qualification` — "How much should AI qualify or hedge...?"
*Examples stay visible inline.*

**Q:** Why would I want hedging at all?
**A:** Because "the date will slip" and "the date will slip if the vendor is late again" are
different claims. Some readers need the second one.

### `peeves` — "What makes you rewrite something on sight?"
**Q:** Why is this question here?
**A:** Because it's the fastest way to describe how you want things written. Saying what annoys you
is easier than describing good writing.

**Q:** What if none of these bother me?
**A:** Then skip it. This one's optional, and an honest blank beats a guess.

### `audiences_list` — "Who do you write to regularly?"
**Q:** What does AI do with this?
**A:** It shifts tone by reader. Your manager and your team get different versions of the same
update.

**Q:** How many should I pick?
**A:** Only the ones you actually write to often. Three or four is typical.

### `audience_variance` — "Do any of these need something different?"
**Q:** What kind of difference counts?
**A:** A different tone, more or less detail, or a different format entirely. Anything you'd
consciously change.

**Q:** What if they're all about the same?
**A:** Then skip it. "Same for everyone" is a real answer and AI will treat it that way.

### `terms_depend_on` — "What terms, tools, or shorthand...?"
**Q:** What counts as shorthand?
**A:** Acronyms, tool names, project codenames — anything you'd have to explain to someone starting
Monday.

**Q:** How much should I list?
**A:** Five to ten is plenty. The ones that come up weekly, not every term you know.

### `never_words` — "Words or phrases you never want to see?"
**Q:** Does this actually work?
**A:** Yes — this is one of the most literal instructions in the file. AI avoids what you list here.

### `standards_list` — "What would you actually send a draft back over?"
**Q:** How is this different from the peeves question?
**A:** Peeves are style. These are hard rules AI can't talk itself out of — the things that make
something wrong, not just annoying.

### `reference_example_primary` — "Paste something you wrote..."
**Q:** Why does this matter so much?
**A:** One real paragraph teaches AI more about your voice than every answer you've given so far.

**Q:** What should I pick?
**A:** Something ordinary you'd be happy to see again. A status update or a note to your team beats
anything polished.

**Q:** What if I can't find one?
**A:** Write two sentences here as if you were sending them. That works just as well.

### `reference_example_second` — "Want to add a second example?"
**Q:** Is one enough?
**A:** Yes. A second one helps most if it's a different tone or a different reader than the first.

## VB-06 · Tone

### The "failure to launch" line — DECIDED
Ships on the Module 11 transition (the "almost there" moment), as the third beat:

> Most people who stop, stop here — with the useful part already answered and the proof still
> ahead. Five more minutes and you'll see it work.

Carries the motivation without implying fault, and names something specific and true — the proof
really is still ahead. Stays inside `GUARDRAILS.md`'s "no nudges framed as guilt," so no guardrail
exception is needed.

### Conversational register — how this is achieved
Not with a mascot, a persona name, or a fake chat UI. It's carried by four things already in this
draft:
1. **Transitions that say why**, not just what's next.
2. **Deep-dives written as the question they'd actually ask** ("What counts as a role?"), not as
   documentation.
3. **Real second person throughout** — "you stop explaining who they are," not "users avoid
   repetition."
4. **Naming the length honestly** ("this one's the longest section") instead of pretending it's
   short. Acknowledging the cost is what buys the trust to keep going.
