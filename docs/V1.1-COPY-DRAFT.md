# V1.1 copy — first draft, for review

Everything new that `docs/V1.1-REFINEMENT.md` depends on. **Nothing here is built yet** — this is the
draft to edit before any of it gets wired up.

Written against `docs/design-system.html` §08 (binding): second person, present tense, active voice;
no product nouns the person didn't bring; sentences under 20 words; small numbers as words; buttons
are verbs they'd say; never apologise, never blame. Target reading grade 7 — `npm run audit` measures
this, so edits should stay in the same register.

Two standing constraints this copy respects, both from `docs/GUARDRAILS.md`:
- **No tour, no coach-marks.** These screens are in-flow narrative — the person is reading the thing
  they're already doing, not being pointed at UI by a dismissible overlay.
- **No nudges framed as guilt.** Your "biggest risk is failure to launch" line is in here, but
  reframed to be about momentum rather than fault. Both versions are shown at VB-06 so you can pick.

## VB-03 · Deeper-dive questions

`Step.deepDive` entries. The rule I applied: a deep-dive earns its place when someone could
reasonably be **stuck on what the question means**. Where the existing `hint` is a short instruction
("Skip if there's only one name for it"), it stays a plain hint — turning an instruction into a
fake question would be worse, not better.

**11 questions get a deep-dive. The other 12 hinted questions keep their hints as-is** (listed at
the end).

### `orientation_ready` — "We're going to build a Context file..."
Your example. The existing hint becomes the answer.

**Q:** What is a Context file?
**A:** A short, plain-text file — a few pages, not a database. You answer questions here, and the
file gets written for you. You download it and hand it to whatever AI you use.

**Q:** Why would AI need this?
**A:** AI starts every conversation knowing nothing about you. This is the part you'd otherwise
retype every time.

### `context_scope` — "work, personal life, or both?"
**Q:** What changes based on my answer?
**A:** Only the wording of later questions. Pick "both" if you're unsure — nothing gets locked out.

### `role_names` — "What are your roles...?"
**Q:** What counts as a role?
**A:** Anything you'd describe differently to different people. A job is a role. So is running a
fundraiser, or coaching a team.

**Q:** What if I only have one?
**A:** That's normal — most people have one or two. Pick one and keep going.

### `responsibilities_list` — "What are you personally responsible for?"
**Q:** How is this different from what I do all day?
**A:** Responsibility is what lands on you if it goes wrong. You do plenty of things you're not
ultimately on the hook for.

### `contribution_boundaries` — "What work do you contribute to but do not own?"
**Q:** Why does AI need to know what I don't own?
**A:** So it stops writing as if a call is yours to make. It changes how things get worded.

### `decision_rights` — "What kinds of decisions can you make yourself?"
**Q:** What if it depends?
**A:** It usually does. Say what you can decide alone, and where the line is. "Under ten thousand,
my call" is a real answer.

### `entities_gate` — "Are there specific people, teams, tools...?"
**Q:** What kind of thing belongs here?
**A:** Anything you name constantly and then have to explain. A manager, a teammate, a tool you
live in, a meeting that matters.

**Q:** Should I add my whole team?
**A:** No. Only the ones AI would need to recognize to be useful. Two or three is plenty.

### `initiatives_gate` — "Are there specific initiatives or projects...?"
**Q:** What makes something an initiative?
**A:** It has a name, a goal, and an end. Your ongoing responsibilities aren't initiatives — those
came up earlier.

### `risk_tolerance` — "fast, good-enough call or slower, more certain one?"
**Q:** What if it depends on the situation?
**A:** Pick "depends on the stakes" — it's a real answer, not a cop-out. AI will ask when it
matters.

### `rigor_before_acting` — "How much reasoning do you want to see...?"
**Q:** What does this actually change?
**A:** Whether AI hands you a two-line answer or walks you through how it got there. You can always
ask for more in the moment.

### `guardrails_list` — "What should AI never do without asking you first?"
**Q:** What happens if I leave these on?
**A:** AI drafts instead of sending, and asks before changing anything. Most people keep all three
on.

**Q:** Can I change these later?
**A:** Yes. Everything here can be answered again — nothing is locked in.

## VB-05 · Module transition screens

One per module, **modules 2–11 only** — module 1 already opens with `orientation_ready` and closes
with the `architecture_orientation` beats, so a transition screen there would be a third welcome in
a row.

Format matches the existing `beats` convention: each `__word__` renders with light underline
emphasis, beats fade in one at a time at reading pace. Two beats each, matching
`ARCHITECTURE_ORIENTATION_BEATS`'s existing shape.

The third line in each is the **example/interactive beat** — grounded in what that module's answers
actually do downstream, shown in a `ReadOnlyBlock`-style presentation so it reads as a real preview,
not decoration.

### → Module 2 · About Me
> Now the basics: __who you are__ and what you actually do.
>
> Not your job title — the version you'd say out loud to someone at a party.

**Preview beat**: *This is what AI reads first, before anything else you tell it.*

### → Module 3 · Responsibilities & Boundaries
> Next: what's __yours__ — and just as usefully, what isn't.
>
> This is the part that stops AI writing as if every call is yours to make.

**Preview beat**: *Two people with the same title can own completely different things. This is where
you say which one you are.*

### → Module 4 · My World
> Now the __names__ — the people, teams, and tools you mention constantly.
>
> Name them once here, and you stop explaining who they are every single time.

**Preview beat, real and concrete**:
> Without this, you write: *"Can you draft a note to Priya — she's my manager, she owns budget
> approvals, and she likes a heads-up before surprises."*
>
> With it, you write: *"Draft a note to Priya."*

### → Module 5 · Initiatives
> This one's the __longest__ section, and the one that pays off most.
>
> These are the named projects AI can actually help you move, not just describe.

**Preview beat**: *Worth the extra few minutes: this is the difference between AI writing about your
work and AI helping with it.*

### → Module 6 · How I Think
> The questions get __faster__ from here. Mostly taps, not typing.
>
> This part is about how you decide things — how fast, how certain, how much you want to be asked.

**Preview beat**: *Five quick picks. Under two minutes.*

### → Module 7 · How I Communicate
> Now your __voice__ — how things should sound when AI writes them for you.
>
> A few of these show you two versions side by side. Pick the one you'd actually send.

**Preview beat**: *This is the section people notice most in the results. It's why AI output stops
sounding generic.*

### → Module 8 · Audience Profiles
> You don't write to __everyone__ the same way.
>
> Your manager and your team get different versions of the same update. This is where you say so.

**Preview beat**: *Two questions.*

### → Module 9 · Vocabulary & Knowledge
> Every workplace has its own __shorthand__.
>
> The acronyms, tool names, and project codenames that would lose an outsider completely.

**Preview beat**: *Teach it these once and you stop spelling them out mid-sentence forever.*

### → Module 10 · Context Boundaries
> The __rules__ AI doesn't get to talk itself out of.
>
> What would make you send a draft back, and what it should never do without asking you first.

**Preview beat**: *These stay on by default. You can turn any of them off, but most people don't.*

### → Module 11 · Reference Examples
> Last one. Paste something you __wrote__.
>
> One paragraph teaches AI more about your voice than every answer you've given so far.

**Preview beat**: *Then you're done — and you get to see the whole thing actually work.*

## VB-06 · Tone

### The "failure to launch" line
Your note: *"Maybe I even want to be specific and explicit that the biggest risk to failure is
failure to launch."*

`GUARDRAILS.md` bans "nudges framed as guilt," so here's the same idea in a register that stays
inside it — plus your original, so you can overrule me deliberately rather than by accident:

**Recommended, on the Module 11 transition (the "almost there" moment):**
> Most people who stop, stop here — with the useful part already answered and the proof still
> ahead. Five more minutes and you'll see it work.

**Alternative, if you want the risk framing explicit:**
> The only way this doesn't work is if you don't finish. You're four questions away.

**Your original phrasing, unedited, if you want it as-is:**
> The biggest risk to failure is failure to launch.

My read: the first is the same motivation without implying fault, and it names a real, specific
thing ("the proof is still ahead") instead of a general warning. The third reads like a slogan
aimed at a founder, not a sentence aimed at the person answering question thirty-one — it's your
voice, but it's not written to this reader.

### Conversational register — how this is achieved
Not with a mascot, a persona name, or a fake chat UI. It's carried by four things already in this
draft:
1. **Transitions that say why**, not just what's next.
2. **Deep-dives written as the question they'd actually ask** ("What counts as a role?"), not as
   documentation.
3. **Real second person throughout** — "you stop explaining who they are," not "users avoid
   repetition."
4. **Naming the length honestly** ("this one's the longest section") instead of pretending it's
   short. Acknowledging the cost is what buys the trust to keep going.
