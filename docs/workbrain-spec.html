Workbrain

A personal AI operating system that lives in a browser side panel, holds nothing on a server, and travels with the person across tools, jobs and years. This document specifies the product, the interaction model, the design language, the company tier, the services attached to it, and an honest assessment of how — and whether — to charge for any of it. Two late additions carry more weight than their length suggests: an audit that reads the person's own chat history to seed their file, and a report that measures, honestly, whether any of this made them better.

01The product in one page

Most people using AI at work are re-explaining themselves every single time they open a chat. Workbrain interviews them once, produces a file that says who they are and how they work, and then keeps that file alive as their world changes. The file is theirs. It goes wherever they go.

What it is
A Chrome side panel that builds and maintains three plain-text files describing a person's working context, their repeatable methods, and what they want automated.
Where it runs
Entirely in the browser. No account, no server, no AI calls of our own. The person's own AI does every piece of generative work, through copy-and-paste or an optional assisted hand-off.
Why a panel
Every step of the flow involves going somewhere else to paste something. A panel stays open across tab switches; a popup closes the moment it loses focus and a website loses the person entirely.
The four steps
Name who you are · Repeat what you do often · Act on real systems · Share with your team. Internally these are gates 1–4; the person never sees the word gate.
The hook
It travels. Change tools, change teams, change jobs — the file comes along, and the parts your employer owns detach cleanly from the parts you own.
The company angle
A team publishes a skill pack. New hires subscribe with one link and inherit the team's methods on day one instead of month three. Nothing personal ever flows upward.
The business
The product is free and stays free. It qualifies and educates people for two human services: AI Coaching for individuals, and TIM — Technology Implementation Management — for teams.

The one-sentence version. Workbrain turns the thing you keep re-explaining to AI into a file you own, keeps that file honest as your job changes, and hands your team a way to pass working knowledge to the next person without a wiki nobody reads.

02Who it is for, and the one constraint that binds everything

The design constraint is not "make it simple." It is: a competent adult who does not think of themselves as technical must be able to finish this alone, on their first try, without asking anyone for help. Every decision in this document is downstream of that sentence.

Three people to design for

WhoWhat they arrive withWhat loses them
The capable non-technical professional
the median user		 Uses ChatGPT most days, gets mediocre results, suspects they are doing it wrong, has never installed an extension. A blank text box. A settings screen. Anything that implies they should already know something.
The skeptical operator Has been burned by tools that phoned home. Will read the permissions prompt. Will ask where the data goes. Any permission they cannot see a reason for. Any claim about privacy that the interface then contradicts.
The team lead with a hiring problem Onboards people constantly, watches the same knowledge get re-learned every time, has no budget line for software. Anything requiring IT approval, a procurement cycle, or a login their team will not adopt.

What "low barrier to entry" actually means here

It is not a value; it is a list of things the product is not allowed to do. Each of these is a real decision that costs capability, and each is worth the cost.

  • No account, ever, for individuals. No email, no password, no verification step. The install is the sign-up.
  • No permission prompt at install beyond storage and the side panel. Host access to AI sites is requested later, in context, only when the person clicks something that needs it — and declining leaves the product fully functional.
  • First real value inside ten minutes. The proof moment — see the same question answered better with your file attached — must be reachable in one sitting.
  • Nothing is required. Every question can be skipped. A file built from four answers is a valid file.
  • No settings screen at launch. Two toggles live in the header. Everything else is a default we chose.
  • No blank boxes where a list would do. Choice pills wherever a reasonable answer set exists.
  • No jargon in the interface. The words in this document are for us. See the design system, section 08.
  • Nothing destructive. Which means nothing needs confirming, which means no modal dialogs at all.

The tension worth naming. Every one of those constraints makes the product weaker as software and stronger as an adopted habit. A login would enable sync, recovery and analytics. We are choosing to lose all three. Section 13 is honest about what that costs.

03The OS model

Three files and a home screen. Everything the product does is either building one of the files, keeping one of them honest, or proving they work.

FileThe person seesHoldsOwned byBuilt by
Context.mdWho you are Scope, roles and their durability, responsibilities, the people and systems worth knowing by name, how you think, how you write, what AI should never do unasked. The person, alwaysContext Interview
Skills.mdHow you work Repeatable methods, written as instructions an AI can follow the same way every time. Yours, plus any your team publishes. Split — personal and team blocks are separately markedSkills Interview + library
Actions.mdWhat should happen automatically What should touch a real system, where that system is, what it would take, and what is honestly out of reach alone. Mostly the employerActions Interview

The home screen is derived, not stored

There is no state machine tracking where someone is. The home screen computes everything it shows from the answers already in storage: how complete each file is, how old, how many parts are past due, what the single next move should be. Change one durability answer and the next move changes with it, immediately, with no migration and nothing to keep in sync.

This matters more than it sounds. A stored progress state is the thing that goes wrong in month four — it drifts out of agreement with the actual data, and the product starts telling people things that are not true about their own file. Derivation cannot drift.

Freshness comes free from the interview

The interview already asks how durable each role is and gives initiatives success criteria. Those are clocks, and until now they were only prose. The specification is that they drive the product:

Answer givenBecomesSurfaces as
Role durability: "a year or so"Review due at ~9 months, flagged at ~5"You said your team-lead role was good for about a year. That was five months ago."
Role durability: "this quarter"Review due at 10 weeksSame sentence, shorter clock
Initiative with success criteriaExpires when criteria are met or the date passes"Your signup funnel initiative — where did it land?"
Any section untouched for 6 monthsSoft flag, no naggingIncluded in the next drift check, never its own alert

Nothing is inferred and nothing is observed. Every freshness signal traces back to a sentence the person typed. This is both the honest implementation and the one we can defend to a skeptical user in a single sentence.

05The lifecycle

The question that decides whether this is a product or a demo: what is happening at week six and month six? Most tools of this shape die quietly at week three, when the initial file has gone stale and there is no reason to open the panel.

WhenWhat the person doesWhat the product doesWhat could kill it
Day 0
install		 Installs, opens the panel, answers the first question inside 60 seconds. Pre-flight is two yes/no questions, not a tour. No permission prompt beyond storage. A scary permission dialog. A wall of text. Asking for an email.
Day 0 + 10 min
first value		 Finishes enough of the interview to generate a file, then runs the proof: same question, with and without. Generates the file, walks the four proof steps, logs the first score. The interview being too long before anything is produced. This is why the file generates from partial answers.
Week 1 Uses the file two or three times. Notices something missing. Capture. One button on home: paste the thing you just had to re-explain, confirm where it goes. Having to re-run an interview to add one line. Capture is the fix.
Week 6
the real test		 Has half-forgotten the tool. Opens the panel because something changed at work. Drift check. Home leads with what is out of date and why, in their own words, and offers two questions — not a re-interview. Opening to a screen that looks the same as week one. Nothing to do, so nothing happens, so they never come back.
Month 3 Role changes, or they take on a project. Durability clocks fire. Capture handles the small stuff. The skills library has new entries since last visit. Silence. This is where a monthly reason to open the panel earns its keep.
Month 6 The file is substantially different from the one they built. They have four or five skills. Re-score. Run the same proof against the same rubric. The number moves, and the movement is the story. No evidence it is still working. The score is the evidence.
Job change Leaves, or joins somewhere new. Take it with you. One action separates personal from employer-owned, and produces a clean file. Losing everything, or worse — walking out with material that belonged to the last employer.

The retention mechanic, stated plainly. There are exactly three legitimate reasons to open this panel after the first week: something changed and I want to record it, something has gone stale and Workbrain told me, or there is a new skill worth taking. Everything else is a notification we have not earned. If those three are not enough, the honest conclusion is that this is a tool people use twice a year — which is still a real product, but a different one, and it should not be dressed up as a daily habit.

06Interaction specification

The guided step

One screen shape carries the whole product. Everything else is a variation of it.

RegionContentsRule
Step label"Question 4 of 40 · About you"Always says where they are and how much is left. Never a percentage alone.
The questionOne sentence, 22px, no more than two linesIf it needs a second sentence, that sentence is the helper text, not the question.
HelperWhy we are asking, or what a good answer looks likeOptional. Never instructions on how to use the interface.
InputPills, or one fieldPills wherever a set of answers exists, always ending in "+ add your own."
Escape"Ask me that a different way"The single most valuable affordance for a non-technical user, because it converts "I don't understand" into a second attempt rather than an exit.
FooterBack · Next · Skip · "Saved on this device"Fixed position. Skip never disabled. The privacy line is permanent, not a one-time notice.

The reflect step

Open-text answers do not commit directly. They are played back verbatim with three choices: keep it, have your own AI tighten it, or say it again. This exists because the fear of writing something wrong is the single biggest source of abandonment in an interview flow — and seeing your own words quoted back, unchanged, is the fastest way to dissolve it.

The cue system

Guidance is declared as data on each step, not written per screen. A step says what to play and what event ends it:

Step definitioncues: [ { play: ['sweep:choices'], until: 'choice', say: 'Pick the AI you use most.' }, { play: ['ring:prompt','sweep:copy'], until: 'copy', say: 'Copy this, and ask it in a fresh chat.' }, { play: ['focus:paste','point:page.composer|Ask it here'], until: 'paste' }, { play: ['ring:next'], until: 'next' } ]

Each link plays until its event fires, then hands off. Adding guidance to a new step is one line of data. The four cue verbs and their reduced-motion equivalents are specified in the design system, section 06.

The invariant: a cue means exactly one instruction and stops the moment it is followed. Nothing loops for decoration. A person who ignores every cue still completes the flow — cues accelerate, they never gate.

The assisted hand-off, and its three postures

The most repeated interaction in the product is copy → switch tab → paste → run → copy → switch back → paste. A content script can shorten it. How far it should is a permissions decision, not a design one, so the product ships all three and lets the person choose:

PostureWhat happensHost permissionDefault
ManualCopy and paste. The panel stays visible, which is already the win over a website.NoneOn install
Fill onlyWorkbrain types the prompt into the message box. The person presses send.Write access to the four AI originsOffered after the first manual round trip
Fill and bring backAlso reads the reply and puts it in the panel.Read access to model outputNever default. Explicit opt-in, per site.

The panel states the cost of the selected posture in plain words, permanently, under the composer — not once in a dialog. "No host permissions · the panel never reads or writes this page" is a sentence a skeptical user can verify by watching.

07Portability — the part that makes it worth starting

The pitch is that this travels with you. That is only true if we build the leaving as carefully as the joining.

Three layers of ownership, made real

LayerExampleOn a job change
PersonalHow you think, how you write, what you never want to see, your durable rolesComes with you, untouched
TeamYour team's status-update method, its review rubric, its escalation rulesDetaches. Marked in the file, removed on request, never silently retained.
EmployerNamed systems, named colleagues, initiative detail, anything in Actions.mdDetaches, and the person is told plainly which parts and why.

The file itself carries the markings — a block written by a team pack is labelled as such in the markdown, so the separation is visible in the artifact and not just in our database (of which there is none).

"Take it with you"

One action on the home screen, offered by name when a role answer changes and always available:

  1. Shows what will be kept and what will be removed, as two short lists in plain words.
  2. Produces a single download — a clean personal file, plus the full original if they want it.
  3. Offers to reset the panel for the new job, or to keep going as-is.

This is also the backup story. There is no cloud copy, so the download is the safety net. The product should nudge a download after any substantial change — not as an error state, as a habit: "Your file changed a lot this month. Worth keeping a copy somewhere."

New device

Import the file back in. The panel reads its own markdown and rebuilds the answers. This means the export format must be round-trippable, which is a real constraint on how the file is generated and should be treated as a hard requirement from the first release, not retrofitted.

Chrome's own sync is not the answer here: chrome.storage.sync caps at roughly 100 KB total and 8 KB per item, which comfortably holds preferences and a pack subscription but not a person's context file. Preferences sync; the file travels as a file.

08Workbrain Connected — the company tier

The claim is that skills and actions become company property that shortens the hiring ramp and spreads good practice across teams. That needs a distribution mechanism, and it needs one that does not break the promise the individual product is built on.

Skill packs

A company publishes a pack: a versioned file at an HTTPS URL containing skills, each with declared slots. A person subscribes by pasting one link, or clicking one the company sent. The panel checks for a new version on a slow schedule and tells the person when the pack has changed — it never updates silently.

The data flows one way only. Packs come down. Nothing goes up — not usage, not answers, not the fact that a given person subscribed. An employer using Workbrain gets their methods distributed; they do not get visibility into any individual's file. This constraint is what makes the tool adoptable by employees at all, and it should be stated in the company-facing material as prominently as in the consumer-facing material.

What it buys the company

ProblemWhat the pack does
Hiring rampA new hire installs, pastes the team link, and on day one their AI already knows how this team writes a status update, runs a review, and escalates. The knowledge that normally takes a quarter to absorb arrives as a file.
Tribal knowledge walking outWhen someone good leaves, the method stays in the pack. This is the argument that gets a budget line, and it is the same argument that made the original Work Brain thesis land.
Best practice not spreadingA person can nominate one of their own skills to the pack. An owner reviews and republishes. Good practice moves sideways without a wiki nobody reads.
Inconsistent outputTen people using the same rubric produce comparable work. The rubric lives in one file, versioned.

Deployment, in order of friction

Zero setup
Paste a link

Works for any team, any company, no IT involvement. This is the default and should stay the default.

Light IT
Managed config

An admin pushes the pack URL through Chrome policy, so the panel arrives pre-subscribed with nothing for the employee to do.

Full IT

Chrome's ExtensionInstallForcelist deploys the extension itself to managed browsers. Supported on AD, Azure AD, MDM or Chrome Enterprise Core, and works with a self-hosted build.

All three paths land in the same product. There is no separate enterprise build to maintain, and no admin console — the pack file is the admin surface, which is a feature, because it can be edited by the person who actually knows the method rather than routed through a tool.

What this deliberately does not do

No seats to manage, no directory integration, no usage reporting, no per-employee dashboard. Every one of those is a real enterprise expectation and every one requires a backend, an account system, and a security posture to match. That is a different company. If demand proves it out, section 15 notes what it would take.

The briefing is the handoff

Instead of an email capture, the person copies a briefing the panel assembles: where they are, what is stale, what their own AI said they should fix, and where they think they are stuck. They paste it into an email or an intake form. The lead arrives pre-qualified and unusually well-informed, and nothing was transmitted to produce it. This inverts the usual trade — the privacy constraint is the reason the lead is good.

The honest cost. With no identity there is no retargeting and no way to re-reach someone who drifts. Volume will be lower than a gated email list would produce. The bet is that a smaller number of people who arrive holding a written account of their own problem converts better than a larger number of addresses.

10Monetization

The question was whether products like this are commonly monetized, and how. The short answer: yes, by four mechanisms, none of which fit this product's individual tier — and one of which fits its company tier and services almost perfectly.

What the market actually does

Google shut down Chrome Web Store Payments on 1 February 2021, so there is no in-store purchase flow to lean on. Everything is handled by the developer, typically through Stripe directly or a wrapper such as ExtensionPay, which exists specifically as the replacement and is not tied to a Google account. Publishing costs a one-time $5 developer registration covering up to 20 extensions.

ModelHow it worksFit here
One-time license keyPay once, get a key, key is validated by an API call. No account needed. Appeals to subscription fatigue.Partial. Works technically and philosophically. But a key check is a server call, which punctures "nothing leaves the browser" unless carefully framed.
Freemium subscriptionCore free, advanced paid, monthly.Poor. Recurring price needs recurring cost or recurring value. There is no API bill here, and the individual value is front-loaded.
Usage creditsBuy credits, spend on AI calls. Common for AI extensions.Not applicable. The person's own AI does all the work. There is nothing to meter.
Team / seat licensingPer-seat or per-org, sold to a buyer rather than a user.Strong. The company tier has ongoing deliverables — pack curation, updates, enablement — that justify a recurring price.

A word on benchmarks. There is no credible public data on extension conversion rates or average revenue in 2026. An older crawl found roughly 4.7% of extensions had any payment support and one-off prices clustered near $0.99, and neither figure means anything now. Any plan built on an assumed conversion rate is built on nothing. The only numbers worth planning against are ones this product measures itself.

The closest analogue

Obsidian is the reference model for a local-first, no-account tool: the app is free with no sign-up, and money comes from adjacent paid services — Sync at $4–5 per user per month, Publish at $8–10 per site per month — plus a $50 per user per year commercial licence that the FAQ openly says is not required, and a $25 one-time supporter licence. The instructive part is not the prices. It is that the free tier is genuinely unlimited and the paid things are all additional, never removals.

What not to do

  • Do not gate the payoff. The Module 12 redesign already removed friction before the proof moment. Putting a price there would undo the product's own best decision.
  • Do not run ads and do not sell data. Beyond the ethics, either one ends the product — the entire proposition is that nothing leaves the browser.
  • Do not charge individuals a subscription. There is no recurring cost to cover and the value is front-loaded. It would read as rent.
  • Do not build seat management before someone asks twice. Pack subscription with no accounts covers the first wave of companies. Accounts are a different company.

The sequencing that matters: services first, because they exist today and need no build; packs second, once three teams have asked; anything else only if the numbers in section 15 say so.

11Trust, permissions and compliance

Permissions, in three tiers

The install prompt is the highest-friction moment in the entire product. Everything possible is moved out of it and requested later, in context, using optional permissions.

TierWhat it grantsRequestedProduct still works without it
0 · Basestorage, sidePanelAt install—
1 · WriteType into the message box on four named AI originsWhen the person clicks "put it in Claude for me"Yes — falls back to copy and paste
2 · Read your own inputSee what the person types into the composer, never the replyOnly if they turn on ongoing measurementYes — measurement runs from exports instead
3 · Read model outputRead the AI's reply back into the panelExplicit, per site, never defaultYes

Tier 2 deserves emphasis because it is routinely conflated with tier 3 and should not be. Reading what a person types is a materially smaller claim than reading what an AI says back, and — as section 14 shows — it is sufficient for every measurement the product wants to make.

Chrome Web Store, as of now

The 2026 policy update, in force since 1 August, tightened two things that bear directly on this product: any data an extension collects must be strictly necessary to its single declared purpose, and all collection must be prominently disclosed regardless of how closely it relates to that purpose. Changes to data handling after install must be disclosed too.

  • Single purpose statement: "Builds and maintains a personal context file for use with AI assistants." Every permission traces to it.
  • Data collection: none. Not minimal — none. That is the cleanest possible position under this policy and it should be defended hard against every future feature request.
  • Disclosure: permanent, in-panel, in plain words. Not a one-time dialog. The line under the composer changes with the selected posture and is always visible.
  • Host permissions: four named origins, optional, requested in context. Never <all_urls>, which raises review scrutiny and frightens exactly the user we are designing for.
  • Privacy policy: required and published, and it will be short, because there is very little to describe.

Third-party terms

Automated submission on someone else's AI service is the one area where the product could plausibly conflict with another company's terms of use. Fill-only — where the person still presses send — is a materially different act from submitting on their behalf. This is the practical argument for fill-only as the default, alongside the trust argument, and it warrants an actual legal read before tier 3 ships.

12Reliability — what breaks, and what happens when it does

A product that must still work in six months has to be designed around its own decay. These are the specific failure modes, in the order they will actually occur.

FailureWhenDesign response
An AI site redesigns its message boxGuaranteed. Repeatedly. Capability probe on load. If the composer cannot be found, the panel silently returns to copy-and-paste and says nothing. It never shows an error the person cannot act on, and it never tries and fails visibly. Selectors live in one versioned map, updatable without a full release.
Service worker is killedConstantly, by designNo meaningful state in memory. Everything that must survive is written to storage on change, not on interval.
Storage limitsHeavy users, year onestorage.local is the home for files; request unlimitedStorage only if a real file approaches the cap. storage.sync holds preferences and the pack subscription only — it caps near 100 KB with an 8 KB per-item limit and cannot hold a context file.
Saved data schema changesEvery meaningful releaseVersioned key, forward-only migrations, and an automatic file export written before any migration runs. If a migration fails, the person still has their file.
The person clears browsing dataEventually, to someoneThe download is the backup, and the product builds the habit early — a gentle prompt to keep a copy after any substantial change. Re-import rebuilds the answers, which is why round-trippable export is a hard requirement.
Browser or platform policy changeAnnuallyNo dependence on anything exotic. Side panel, storage, optional host permissions — the boring, well-supported surface.
The person forgets the product existsWeek threeThe most likely failure of all, and the only one that is a design problem rather than an engineering one. Section 05 is the answer; if it does not work, nothing else here matters.

The governing principle: every enhancement degrades to the manual path, silently. Fill-only failing means copy-and-paste. Pack fetch failing means the last good pack. Export parse failing means the interview still runs. There is no state in which the product is broken — only states in which it is doing less.

13The history audit — starting from evidence instead of a blank page

Every person arriving at this product has already written the answers, hundreds of times, in their own chat history. The audit reads that history — theirs, on their machine, at their invitation — and turns it into the first draft of their file.

The mechanism: their export, not our scraper

Every major AI platform ships a data export. The person requests it, receives it, and drags the file into the panel. This is worth stating plainly because the alternative is so much worse: scraping the history out of the page would need broad read permissions, would break every time a site redesigns, would sit awkwardly against other companies' terms, and would frighten precisely the user we are trying to reach. The export path needs no additional permission at all.

PlatformHow they get itWhat arrives
ChatGPTSettings → Data controls → Export data A zip by email — link expires in 24 hours — containing conversations.json plus a readable chat.html. Each conversation carries a title, create and update timestamps, and a mapping tree of messages by role. Usually a few megabytes even for heavy users.
ClaudeSettings → account-level exportStructured conversation data with metadata. Processing time varies.
Geminitakeout.google.com → deselect all → Gemini AppsHTML or JSON through Google Takeout.
CopilotMicrosoft privacy dashboardLess well documented; treat as a later parser, not a launch one.

One structural gift in the ChatGPT format. Messages are stored as a tree, not a list, because edits and regenerations create branches. Every branch point is a moment the person was not satisfied with what they got. That is a correction signal we get for free, without interpreting a single word.

What the audit looks for, all of it locally and none of it with AI

SignalHow it is foundBecomes
Repeated explanations Normalised 5–8 word shingles across the opening message of every conversation; keep any that recur in three or more separate conversations. Candidate lines for what you keep re-explaining and for boundaries and preferences.
Corrections User turns opening with corrective markers — "no,", "actually", "I meant", "not quite", "too long", "shorter", "I said" — plus every edit/regeneration branch. The highest-value candidates of all. Each correction is a preference that was missing from context.
Names worth knowing Capitalised token sequences appearing mid-sentence three or more times, minus a stoplist. Pre-fills the people, teams and systems block — the section people otherwise skip.
Topics you keep returning to Similarity clustering over conversation titles and opening messages, grouped by day. Candidate initiatives, and a count: "you came back to this on six separate days."
Setup tax In each opening message, the characters of context typed before the first actual request. The baseline number that section 14 measures against.
Vocabulary Terms frequent in their history and rare in general use. Jargon the file should teach their AI.

What the person actually experiences

  1. Home offers it once: "Already used AI a lot? I can read your own chat history and give you a head start."
  2. Three steps to get the export, written as three steps, with the exact menu names.
  3. They pick the file. A progress line, then a single screen: "I found 11 things you explain over and over."
  4. Each one is a tick box, quoted in their own words, with a count: "'I write for operators, not engineers' — you said this in 23 chats."
  5. They untick what is wrong. Everything ticked pre-fills the interview, which now starts two-thirds answered.

This is the single largest accessibility win available to the product. The blank box is where non-technical users stop, and the audit replaces the blank box with a list of things they already said. It converts the hardest question in the product — how do you work? — into the easiest one: is this true?

Rules that are not negotiable

  • Parsed in memory, never stored. The raw export never touches storage. Only the candidates the person ticks are saved, in their edited form.
  • Never transmitted. Not to us, not to their AI, not anywhere. If an optional step uses their AI to tidy the candidates, it sends the extracted lines only — visible in full, in the panel, before they copy it.
  • Their history contains other people. Pasted emails, client details, colleagues' names. This is the strongest argument for the two rules above, and it should be said out loud in the interface, not buried in a policy.
  • Discardable in one action. A visible "forget this file" that clears everything derived, at any point.
  • Optional, always. The interview works perfectly without it and must never imply otherwise.

Engineering constraints

Parse in a worker so a large export never freezes the panel. Stream rather than JSON.parse the whole file where the format allows. Write one parser per platform behind a common interface, version them, and fail soft — an unreadable file produces "I couldn't read that one. Here's how to get a fresh export," never an error code. Assume every format changes without notice; none of them owe us stability.

14The improvement report — what can honestly be claimed

The goal is a sentence a person can say out loud: here is how I am better, and here is why it would have been worth paying for. That sentence is reachable, but only if we are disciplined about which parts of it are measured and which are inferred.

Why this product can do something most cannot. Almost no tool can measure its own before, because the before happened prior to install. Here the chat export contains months of it. That is a genuine structural advantage, and it is the whole reason this feature is worth building.

Two kinds of evidence, and they are not equal

The proof loopThe history trend
What it isSame prompt, same day, once without the file and once with. Graded by their own AI against a fixed rubric.Behaviour before installing versus behaviour after, from the export and from ongoing use.
DesignA within-subject controlled comparison. Same person, same question, same model, minutes apart.Observational. Uncontrolled.
SupportsA causal claim: attaching this file changed the answer.A descriptive claim: this is what changed.
WeaknessOne question, one moment. Says nothing about a month of work.Cannot separate the file from everything else that changed.

Both belong in the report, labelled as what they are. The product's credibility rests on never letting the second one wear the first one's clothes.

The metrics

MetricDefinitionSourceHow solid
Setup tax Median characters of context typed before the actual request, per new conversation. Export for the baseline; what they type for the ongoing figure. Strong. Deterministic, same measure on both sides, no interpretation.
Correction rate Corrective turns plus edit/regeneration branches, per conversation. Export, and composer text going forward. Strong. The marker list is crude but applied identically before and after.
Things you no longer say Of the repeated explanations the audit found, how many appeared in the last 30 days. Both. Strongest. Named, specific, and countable. Ideally zero.
Re-asks per topic Distinct days on which the same topic was raised again. Export. Moderate. Clustering is fuzzy; report as a range, not a figure.
Turns to done Median exchanges per conversation. Export. Weak proxy. A conversation can end because it worked or because they gave up. Show it, caveat it.
Rubric score Their own AI's grade, with and without, five criteria. The proof loop. Strong on design, soft on instrument. Controlled comparison, subjective grader.

On the ratio. Context characters as a share of total prompt is expressible, but it moves for the wrong reasons — a short question makes the ratio look terrible with the same amount of setup. The absolute setup tax is the better number, and it converts directly into the unit that persuades: typing that number of characters at a normal pace, times the number of conversations, is minutes per week.

The measurement needs the cheap permission, not the scary one

Every metric above except turns-to-done can be computed from what the person types. None require reading what the AI says back. This is a fortunate alignment: the most valuable feature in this section runs at permission tier 2, and the tier that frightens people is not needed for it. Say so, in the interface, at the moment consent is asked.

The report

A single screen and a single downloadable file, offered at month one, three and six — and always available. Its structure:

Workbrain report · shapeBefore Workbrain, across 7 months and 214 conversations · you typed about 310 characters of background before asking anything · you corrected the answer 1.8 times per conversation · 11 explanations came up again and again Since then, across 6 weeks and 58 conversations · 40 characters of background — 270 fewer, every time · 0.4 corrections per conversation · 9 of those 11 explanations: not typed once Roughly 25 minutes a week you are not spending re-explaining yourself. At the hourly figure you entered, that is about $—— a month. Measured directly, on the same day, same question: without your file 2.4 / 5 · with your file 4.6 / 5, graded by your own AI What this is not: a controlled study. Your work changed, the models changed, and you have probably got better at this yourself. This is what changed — not proof of what caused it.

Rules for the report

  • The person supplies the hourly number, or it is omitted. Never assume a rate, never pick one for them, never show a dollar figure they did not author. An invented ROI number destroys the credibility of every real number beside it.
  • No composite index. No Workbrain Score out of 100. A made-up aggregate is the fastest way to look like every other dashboard and the fastest way to be disbelieved.
  • Confounds are printed on the report, not in a footnote. Saying it first is what makes the rest believable — and anyone who would be persuaded by this number is someone who will think of the objection themselves.
  • Zero and negative results are shown. If nothing improved, the report says so and offers the drift check. A measurement tool that can only report success is a marketing asset, not a measurement tool.
  • Free, like everything else. This is the artifact that makes someone buy coaching or expense a pack. Charging for it would be charging for the sales pitch.

Who the report is for

ReaderWhat convinces them
The personMinutes a week, and the named things they no longer type. Concrete, personal, theirs.
Whoever they are asking to payThe same figures plus the controlled comparison — and the fact that the confounds are stated. This is the document that gets coaching expensed.
A companyRamp time and consistency, assembled only from reports individuals chose to share. Nothing aggregates automatically, because nothing flows upward. A manager can ask; the product will never tell.

The report also solves a problem from section 05: it gives month three and month six a reason to exist. Running it again is a ritual with a result, which is precisely what a maintenance product needs and rarely has.

15Instrumentation without an account

Having refused analytics, we should be clear-eyed about what we can and cannot see, and resist the temptation to quietly reintroduce it.

What we can seeWhat we cannot
Chrome Web Store installs, uninstalls and weekly active usersWhere in the interview people abandon
Store ratings and written reviewsWhich questions confuse people
Requests for team packs, and pack fetch volume by URLWhether anyone completed anything
Briefings that arrive as emails or intake submissionsHow many were generated and not sent
Whatever people tell us in coaching sessionsAnything about the silent majority

The store's weekly-actives figure is the honest north star, and briefings received is the honest conversion metric. Everything else has to come from talking to people — which for a product whose paid tier is human services is not the handicap it would be elsewhere.

The one legitimate exception is a local diagnostic the person can read and choose to send: the panel already computes everything about its own use for the report. A "send this with my briefing" tick box turns that into research, with consent, at the moment they were already sharing. That is the only telemetry that should ever exist here.

16Build sequence

Ordered so that each release is independently useful, and so the riskiest assumption gets tested first.

Release 1
Parity, well made

Side panel, the Context interview panel-native, the file, the proof loop, manual hand-off only. No host permissions at all.

Release 2
The reason to return

Home surface, freshness from durability answers, drift check, capture, take-it-with-you export and re-import.

Release 3
Evidence

History audit with the ChatGPT parser, interview pre-fill, and the first improvement report.

Release 4
Reach

Fill-only hand-off as an optional permission. Skills interview, library page, team packs.

Later
Only if earned

Actions interview, further parsers, tier-3 read-back, managed deployment, anything with a seat in it.

What each release is testing

ReleaseThe question it answersKill signal
1Will people finish an interview in a panel, and does the proof land?They install and never reach the proof.
2Does anyone come back at week six?Weekly actives collapse after week two and the drift check does not move them.
3Does starting from their own history remove the blank-page problem — and can we show real improvement?The audit finds nothing people recognise, or the numbers do not move.
4Will a team actually publish a pack, and will their people subscribe?Managers like the idea and never author one. This is the likeliest failure in the whole plan.

Release 1 checklist

  • Manifest: single purpose statement, storage and sidePanel only, published privacy policy, no host permissions.
  • Round-trippable export from day one. Retrofitting this later is expensive and it is the entire backup story.
  • Versioned storage schema with a migration path and a pre-migration file export.
  • Every string checked against the plain-language rules and a grade-7 target.
  • Full keyboard pass and a screen reader pass before submission, not after.
  • Reduced-motion equivalents for every cue, verified visually.

17Risks and open questions

RiskAssessment
Nobody comes backThe central risk. Three legitimate return reasons are specified; if none of them pulls people back, the honest conclusion is that this is a twice-a-year tool and should be sold as one rather than dressed as a habit.
No team ever authors a packLikely. Curation is work, and the person who knows the method is rarely the person with time. This is precisely what TIM exists to sell, so the failure mode has a business answer — but do not build pack infrastructure before a team has actually asked twice.
The audit finds nothing recognisablePossible for light users. Mitigation: it is optional, it is offered once, and the interview is complete without it. It should never be the only path in.
The numbers do not moveThen say so. A report that can only show improvement is worthless, and this product's whole position is that it tells the truth about itself.
Store review frictionManageable at tier 0 and tier 1. Tier 3 will attract real scrutiny under the 2026 limited-use rules, which is another reason to ship it last, if at all.
No retargeting, everAccepted, deliberately. Section 15 is the full accounting.
Someone builds this with an account and outruns usThey will, and they will win on capability. The counter-position is the one thing they cannot copy without abandoning their model: nothing you write here goes anywhere. Do not compete on features that require giving that up.

Still genuinely open

  • Does the drift check fire on a schedule, or only when the panel is opened? A scheduled nudge needs a notification surface the product does not currently have, and notifications are the fastest route to an uninstall.
  • What happens on a second machine before re-import becomes a habit — is a "you look new here, do you have a file?" prompt helpful, or unsettling?
  • How much of a pack can a person edit before it stops being the team's method?
  • Does the Actions interview have a useful ending for someone who answers "not sure" twice, other than an offer of help?
  • Is the improvement report shareable as a link, which would require hosting, or only as a file — and does a file get used?
  • Where does the civic and after-school work attach? The free tier makes it possible; the specification does not yet describe it.
