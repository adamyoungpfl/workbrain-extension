## What the references actually are (frames pulled and studied)

**Option 1 — the "Fragment" hero** (dribbble 2739224): a glowing central mark with a field of
angular shard-windows, each holding an image, tumbling slowly in the mark's gravity with
parallax and bloom. It is an ambient HERO; Adam's spec adds the sequence: shards accelerate →

**Option 2 — the liquid film** (dribbble 25209919): ~24s of photoreal 3D-rendered
liquid/glass — swirling vortex, glass arcs, droplets, a glossy sphere, a white ring finale.
This is BAKED OFFLINE RENDER (Cinema4D/Houdini class), not motion graphics code can redraw.
"Recolor and change the endpoint" therefore means producing a video, not styling one.

## The law that shapes both (nothing new — just applied)

1. **We never ship the references' pixels.** Both are another artist's work. Option 1 is
   rebuilt IN STYLE from scratch (fully honest — it's our imagery, our mark, our motion).
   Option 2's finish requires producing a film: license/commission the artist, commission any
   motion designer, or AI-generate — then bundle OUR file.
2. **Everything ships in the bundle** (MV3, no CDN). Assets are install weight. Budgets below.
3. **Photography needs licenses.** People-in-business imagery = licensed stock or AI-generated
   with clean commercial terms. Adam sources/approves; a cost line, not a code line.
4. **Reduced motion carries the instruction**: instant composed end-frame (mark, tagline,
   button, ten-count). Built into the shared rig; non-negotiable.
5. **The white "blink" ships as a soft luminance swell (~340ms up, ~160ms out)** — never a
   hard strobe (photosensitivity, and taste). The prototype shows the swell.
6. **The 10s auto-hand-off is a deliberate return of VB-34's self-ending** — recorded as
   such. VB-73 removed self-ending because the surface carried choices; it is a SHOW again,
   and a show that ends and hands over is a movie, not a door slamming. Once-per-session law
   unchanged, so the cost is paid once per browser session. Any click still exits instantly
   at every moment, including mid-show.

## Option 1 — the gravity of the work brain (build in-panel, live)

The right implementation is REAL-TIME canvas, not video: the shards can genuinely drift until
the person opens the panel (alive, never the same twice), and the whole thing weighs ~1–2MB.

| Piece | What it takes | Lift |
|---|---|---|
| Shard engine | Canvas 2D: 24–30 clipped polygons, textured, gravity spiral, parallax, accel curve, faked motion blur. Same house craft as the wall painter + markOrbit. | 1–1.5 days |
| "Video" shards | Ken Burns pan/zoom INSIDE the shard masks — reads as the Marvel-intro motion at 400px, costs kilobytes. Real video textures possible but +10–40MB and codec risk; recommended against (or exactly one ≤2s loop later). | included |
| The photoreal mark | Our icosahedron as a glossy centerpiece: pre-rendered glow/gloss layers under the live vector graph (2–4 PNGs). A fully photoreal 3D logo render is an asset task (Blender/AI), half a day to produce + integrate. | 0.5 day |
| Shard imagery | 16–24 licensed or AI-generated stills (people at work, spreadsheets, reports, statements), art-directed to one grade, compressed to ~512px WebP ≈ 40–70KB each. **Adam sources or approves the gen batch.** | 0.5–1 day + $0–300 stock |
| Choreography & polish | Wire into the shared rig; tune accel/density/glow to taste (this is where the "professional finished" lives — expect 2–3 taste rounds with Adam). | 1 day |
| Tests | Extend splash.spec: frame probes, RM stills, texture presence, perf floor. | 0.5 day |

**Total: ~3.5–4.5 build days across 3 slices + the shared rig. Bundle +1–2MB. Risk: taste
rounds on the photoreal grade; perf is not a risk (30 sprites is nothing).**
Bonus: a screen-recording of this splash IS the store promo and the site hero.

## Option 2 — the liquid film (produce a video, bake it in)

In-panel code cannot reach that reference's finish (photoreal fluid = offline render). The
professional path: **produce a 4–6s film once, bundle it** (muted webm at panel resolution,
2–6MB), play on open, render the lockup/tagline/button as real DOM over the final seconds
(crisp text, our tokens, real button), same rig underneath.

| Piece | What it takes | Lift |
|---|---|---|
| The film | One of: (a) license/commission the original artist (their piece, recolored, our ending — cleanest match, typical $1–4K + turnaround), (b) any motion designer from a tight brief (same range), (c) AI video gen (Veo/Runway) + grade — cheap but a quality lottery, expect many pulls to land one professional take. | external: days–weeks + $ |
| Integration | Bundled `<video muted playsinline>`, end-frame DOM overlay, RM = poster end-frame, degradation if video fails to decode = straight to composed end state (silent, per the law). | 0.5–1 day |
| Tests | Video-present/RM/degradation/hand-off in splash.spec. | 0.5 day |

**Total in-house: ~1–1.5 build days + the shared rig — but the finish quality lives entirely
in the external production step.** Bundle +2–6MB. Risk: an outside dependency owns the wow.

## The honest comparison

- **Option 1** is the only one that's fully OURS end-to-end, it's alive (real-time, subtly
  different every open), it tells the product's actual story — the shards of a working life
  pulled into the brain's gravity — and it doubles as the marketing capture. More build days,
  more taste rounds, needs an imagery pass.
- **Option 2** is beautiful and cheap in-code but outsources its soul: the finish is bought,
  static, and heavier in the bundle. Strong if Adam wants THAT exact cinematic register and
  is happy to commission it.
- **They stack**: the shared rig ships first either way. Option 1 can ship on the rig now and
  an Option-2-style film could still replace/augment it later without rework.
