## The three sub-slices

Each lands behind a full `npm run check`, one commit each, per CLAUDE.md.

### 4a — the cluster · BUILT 2026-09-01

The baseline door becomes two doors that read as one object: narrated (the
heavy lean) and silent. Both go to the same place; which one is pressed sets
the narrator preference on the way in.

**The splash's own `NarratorToggle` goes with it.** A screen that asks "with
the voice or without" and *also* carries a mute toggle is asking twice — the
same objection BR-01 made when it deleted the third control from this screen.
The toggle is not lost: it lives in the interview header, which is where
somebody changes their mind.

**Where there is no speech engine there is no choice**, and the cluster
collapses to the single door it was. `NarratorToggle` already works this way
("not a disabled button, not a note explaining itself"); a cluster offering a
choice the device cannot honour would be worse than the toggle it replaced.

**What removing the toggle broke, and how.** `setPref` returns early when the
value it is handed matches the one in memory, and it was the toggle that
loaded the stored answer into memory. With it gone, memory held the default
`false` — so somebody who had the narrator on and pressed "Set it silently"
wrote nothing. The cluster loads preferences on mount and awaits that load
inside the press. Worth knowing before anything else on this screen starts
reading a preference: **the splash has no other reader.**

### 4b — the cable and the pulse · BUILT 2026-09-01

The launch door becomes a control-panel button with a cable hanging from it,
faintly lit. Pressing it sends a pulse up the cable into the button, and the
pulse is what starts 4c.

`core/splash/launch.ts` holds both clocks: `pulseAt` (where the light is, 420ms
end to end, accelerating because current arrives fastest where it lands) and
`idleGlowAt` (the wire's slow breath at rest, small enough not to compete with
the doors it sits under).

**Two things to know before touching this again.**

*The press is gated on a running loop, not on a copy of the loop's guards.*
`press` used to re-test `still` and the ref from outside the effect, which
missed the case where the effect bails because the cable reports no length —
a press in that state armed a pulse nothing was running and the door never
opened. `wired.current` is set by the loop that does the work.

*`.home` is visible from the first paint, underneath the splash.* Any test
timing the hand-off has to wait for `.splash` to go, not for `.home` to
arrive. A test that waited for Home measured Playwright's round trip and
asserted 420ms against it.

### 4c — the rocket · BUILT 2026-09-01

The rocket materialises, rumbles, and leaves upward; everything else fades to
the dark field; a white trail becomes the whiteout that hands over to Home.

`core/splash/rocket.ts` is the whole flight as one clock — `LAUNCH_MS` (the
1.6s of assumption #2) with every beat inside it. `SplashRocket.tsx` draws it;
`Splash.tsx` owns the seam: the doors call `launch`, not `leave`, and the
flight's end calls `leave` with the destination the press parked in a ref.

**Under reduced motion there is no rocket** and the hand-off is immediate,
which is what the splash already does. The law is unchanged: the still version
keeps the instruction, and the instruction here is "you are in".

**Four decisions the next person should not have to re-derive.**

*"Everything else fades" is one layer, not five.* The rocket's stage is the
dark field itself, fading in over the reveal — the doors, sections and lockup
sink together, and a stage that fails to paint leaves a working screen
underneath. The reveal is `inert` for the flight (`.splash-hold`): its doors
are still in the DOM under an opaque stage, and an invisible button that still
took an Enter could re-choose narration mid-air.

*The hand-off does not depend on the drawing.* `onDone` fires from the paint
loop when it runs and from a plain timer when it does not. A launch that
cannot draw itself costs the cinema, never the arrival.

*Escape mid-flight finishes the transition to the chosen door* — not to Home.
The person already answered; a shortcut that changed their answer would be the
screen overruling them. Before any press it still means Home, as ever.

*The whiteout is total at `LAUNCH_MS`, by test.* The splash's ordinary 320ms
exit fade starts from that frame — white, into Home — and a whiteout short of
1 would put a dark edge in the cut.

Filming it: `node scripts/film.mjs --launch` presses the launch key (pulse
included); `--baseline` presses the loud door, same rocket without the pulse.

### Built 2026-09-01, and what the next person should know

**The fog fixes the seam by ORDERING, not by covering it.** The destination
is opened at the whiteout (`onWhiteout` → App's `onBaseline`, which no longer
ends the splash; the splash always ends itself via `onDone`), the fog clears
onto it, and the splash unmounts last. `tests/e2e/splash.spec.ts` counts
naked-Home frames in-page and requires zero.

**The fog's clock anchors to the first PAINTED frame after the whiteout, and
holds solid white first (`DISSOLVE_HOLD_MS`).** Two real failure modes, both
hit while building it: the interview's mount can block the main thread
between two ticks (a wall-anchored fog loses exactly those frames), and the
interview renders `null` until `wb:answers` loads (fog that starts during
the read clears onto Home). White held is indistinguishable from white
animating — the hold is where both hide.

**The doors became parts.** `RevealPart` gained `baseline` and `launch`
(`doors` is gone); they arrive on their own beats and settle leaning -12/+16,
carrying the sections' alternation down the screen. The two new links are
gradient squiggles in the reveal's own `defs`; the cable is rebuilt at mount
from a measurement (key to panel corner, settle lean compensated from core's
own table) with a gradient tail-fade — Adam's "fade to black" alternative,
applied always.

**The cluster is typography in a glow.** Heading strings are split
lead/span like the sections' (`splashBaselineLead`/`Span`), and the whole
heading stays in `splashBaseline` because it is the door's accessible name —
an e2e pins `lead + " " + span === whole`.

### Built 2026-09-01/02, and the judgement calls a next person needs

**The elimination reads "Anything → Nothing", not "→ Everything".** The
message's literal words would end the device on "Everything leaves your
browser" — the inverse of the promise the product rests on — so it is read
as a slip. Two beats now, his "tighter on timing"; one string array and
`CLAIM_WORDS` to change if the intent was different.

**The countdown and the flight are ONE length (3s), and the rocket lives.**
The launch brief says the countdown takes the screen to white and never
mentions the rocket — but a 3-2-1 count is what happens BEFORE a launch, so
the flight was retimed to the count (`LAUNCH_MS = COUNTDOWN_MS = 3000`,
asserted equal by test) and the digits ride over it, mission-control style.
The baseline key has no flight: the count's own `white` ride does it. Both
land on the identical full-white frame at zero; the fog needs no knowledge
of which key was held. If Adam wants the rocket gone entirely, it is the
`flight` prop's two call sites.

**Holding is the new press, and a plain click arms under reduced motion.**
The charge is choreography; the CHOICE is the load-bearing part. Keyboard
holds work (Space/Enter down…up, same clock). A tap under full motion
deliberately does nothing — the hold is the confirmation this product
refuses dialogs for.

**The narrated-or-silent choice went back to a toggle**, because one
circular key cannot be two doors — Adam asked for the toggle by name
("button sound on/off"). It is the interview header's own `NarratorToggle`
(one preference, loads-then-writes, hides without a speech engine), first in
the tab order per the 2026-09-02 precedent. The digits are spoken through
it. `splashBaselineSilent` is retired in strings.ts, recorded not deleted.

**The sketch is drawn in ink, not filtered.** Desaturating the pastel set
made fog — the grounds share one light luminance. `sketchTextures()` hands
the painters a gray ramp in place of the wheel, so dark panes carry white
strokes and light panes dark ones. The splash tint cycle is gone (his
"changing of images, not color"); the opening stillness (panels pinned to
their first picture) survives. The app wall (`WallPanels`) draws the same
textures faint under its VB-97 axe cap — the cap is why the light version
whispers; raising `WALL_OPACITY_MAX` is the one constant if Adam wants it
louder, and it is a decision against a recorded readability rule.

**Why the lines got shorter.** The radio originals ("Tower is clear for
systems check." / "All systems go. Commence launch sequence.") overran
the 1.6s hold and were cut mid-word by the counted "3" — a glitch, not
radio. Trimmed to fit; the originals sit in strings.ts's comment and
HOLD_MS is the one number to raise if they are wanted whole. Same story
one beat later: "We have liftoff." did not fit the 0.6s breath between
the spoken "1" and the whiteout, and "Liftoff." does.

**ElevenLabs is a recording tool here, not a runtime dependency.** The
guardrails rule a live call out three ways at once — no feature that only
works online, never transmit anything, no server of ours to hold a key.
So the plan the registry encodes: once interview copy is final-approved,
generate each cue (plus the three digits, plus any sound effects) ONCE
and ship them as bundled clips; `cues.ts` is both the wiring point and
the list of what to record.

**And one engine fix the pass surfaced:** `stopSpeaking()` on an engine
that never spoke now costs nothing. The first touch of `speechSynthesis`
— even a bare `cancel()` — stalls the main thread ~170ms while the voice
list loads, and the splash's skip button was billing that stall to its
own door (the reduced-motion press-cost test caught it at 591ms against a
420ms ceiling).
