# AI Foundations — the ten-minute script

For a fifteen-minute meeting: two minutes of hello, ten of this, three
for their questions. The price is on the title slide on purpose — you
never have to "get to it," and they never wonder when the sell starts.
Deliver it level. The tone is *take it or leave it*; the moment it
sounds like a funnel, the whole posture collapses.

Times are cumulative targets. If you're past 6:00 at slide 10, cut
slide 11 (the two defaults) — it's the one beat the close survives
without.

---

**TITLE — 0:00**
"Ten minutes, and the price is already on the screen — forty-eight
hundred, one cohort, sixteen to twenty of your people, and they never
pay a cent. I'm not going to circle back to it dramatically. I'm going
to tell you what it buys and why it matters, and you'll say yes or no
and either answer is fine."

**01 · Hope this email finds you ___ — 0:40**
"Finish the sentence. …You already did — before I asked. That reflex
you just felt? That's the entire technology. The AI your people
adopted this year is that reflex at scale: for every word it writes,
it continues with what most people would say."

**02 · It always bets on average — 1:20**
"Which isn't a flaw — it's the definition. The most likely word is the
most common word. So every draft it hands your people starts life as
the most common version of the thought. Remember that; everything else
tonight follows from it."

**03 · And it reads them the same way — 2:00**
"Here's the part almost nobody talks about. It doesn't just write like
the average — it READS like the average. Hand it your analyst's
request and it hears the most likely meaning, the average asker's
meaning. What it misreads is exactly what you pay her for: her
judgment, her shorthand, the thing she knows that the average person
doesn't. And it never says 'I misread you.' It gives a confident
answer to a question nobody asked. One study moved measured accuracy
seventy-six points just by rewording. Nature published that these
models systematically misread non-standard dialects. It does not read
all of your people equally well."

**04 · Cookies — 3:10**
"You've seen this movie. Twenty years of web cookies: a file about
you, written by someone else, for someone else's goals. Nobody liked
being profiled. We regulated it."

**05 · The sequel — 3:40**
"Every one of your people already has the sequel. AI memory. On by
default on every major service. Inferred, not written by them — and it
rewrites itself; OpenAI literally calls the process 'dreaming.' At
Microsoft it's exempt from retention policy and not audit-logged —
that's their documentation, not my opinion. Company work is being
drafted through profiles nobody in your organization authored,
audited, or can read in full."

**06 · Three questions — 4:30**
"Don't take my word. Tonight, on your own account, ask your assistant
these three questions. What do you remember about me — count what's
flat wrong. Describe how I communicate — see if you recognize the
person. What matters most in my work — and watch it answer with
everyone's answer. Whatever it does to you, it's doing to each of your
people, every day, silently. The where-to-look guide is on the last
slide; take a photo of it now if you like."

**07 · The honest ledger — 5:20**
"To be fair to the machine: that memory is genuinely good at the
mundane. Zero effort, remembers the recurring stuff, updates itself.
Concede all of it. What it cannot do: confirm anything with the
person, tell anyone it rewrote itself, move to another vendor, or read
what's atypical about someone as anything but noise. A memory nobody
authored is a lens nobody can focus."

**08 · Individually faster, collectively identical — 6:10**
"And the measured group effect: AI assistance makes each person
better and the group more alike. Science Advances, twenty twenty-four.
Ten proposals start reading like one proposal written ten times."

**09 · The day a client feels the template — 6:50**
"The gains are real right now — I'm not telling you they aren't. They
invert the first time a customer recognizes the cadence. And the
discount lands on your brand, not on the tool."

**10 · The same model — 7:20**
"One question to hold: your competitor's proposal was written by the
same model as yours. What exactly is the difference? Your next client
is already asking it."

**11 · The two defaults — 7:50** *(cut this beat if behind)*
"Most organizations pick one of two defaults. Ban it — and people use
it anyway, on personal phones, in the dark. Or let it run — and
everyone converges on the house style. Both are losses."

**12 · The third way — 8:20**
"The managed answer is almost boring: each person writes their own
context — their names, their judgment, their voice — in a plain-text
file they own, and hands it to whatever assistant they already use.
Nothing routes through us. The machine finally reads and writes from
who they are instead of the average."

**13 · Numbers, not testimonials — 8:50**
"And you don't have to believe a word of this on trust. There's a
five-minute test with four checks and no judgment call in it. Every
participant runs a before-and-after on their own work, and you get the
numbers with response rates attached. The whole standard is public at
myworkbrain-dot-org slash good — with the six claims we invite people
to attack."

**14 · The offer — 9:20**
"So, the number from slide one. Forty-eight hundred funds one cohort:
four evenings in your building, sixteen to twenty of your people,
facilitation, materials, loaner devices, certificates, and follow-up
reporting at ninety and one-eighty days. They never pay. And the tool
is free forever after — no license, no seat fee, nothing that expires
when we leave."

**15 · The close — 9:45**
"One decision. If it's for your people, the request form is at
myworkbrain-dot-org slash civic and the packet comes back in two
business days. If it's not — keep the three questions, run them at
your next staff meeting, and I'm glad you gave me the ten minutes.
There's no follow-up sequence. Thanks."

---

**In the three minutes after:** answers to the likely pushbacks are
appendix slides A and B — go there rather than improvising. If they
ask "why should I believe the homogenization stuff," concede its
frontier is early and pivot to what's measurable in their own shop
(slides 06 and 13). Never argue slide 08; it's a citation, not a hill.
