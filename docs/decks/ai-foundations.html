01 · The trick behind the magic

The cat sat on the  ___ 

An AI assistant is autocomplete at scale: it predicts the most likely next word, learned from everyone's writing at once. That's a toy description — and the toy captures the one thing that matters.

Yes, it's a simplification. It's the honest kind: the kind an expert in the room nods at.

02 · The objective, plainly

The machine always bets on average.

Not a bug. Not a defect. It is the definition of the objective: the most likely word is, by construction, the most common one. Everything else in this deck follows from that sentence.

03 · You've seen this movie

Cookies: a file about you,
written by someone else.

For twenty years the web kept a profile of you that you never wrote, rarely saw, and couldn't edit — built for someone else's goals. We regulated it, blocked it, and mostly learned to hate it.

04 · The sequel

AI memory is the super cookie.

  • On by default on all five major services surveyed.
  • Inferred, not authored — and it rewrites itself over time (OpenAI calls the process "dreaming").
  • At Microsoft: exempt from retention policy, not audit-logged, not restrictable — their own documentation.
  • At Perplexity Enterprise: your memories belong to the organization, not you.

Every receipt cited in "Durable Context" (Young, 2026) — myworkbrain.org/#white-paper. A reflection of you, shaped by a company with a vested interest in how your character is drawn.

06 · The quiet risk

Everyone's average.

The evidence so far: AI assistance lifts individual output while shrinking collective diversity — we each get better, and we all get more alike. Models trained on model output drift toward the center. The most likely becomes, more and more, the only.

Doshi & Hauser, Science Advances 2024 · Padmakumar & He, ICLR 2024 · Shumailov et al., Nature 2024. This is a measured trajectory, not a prophecy — and it is early. We say so.

07 · What it costs

The day they feel
the template.

Right now, AI makes you faster — and maybe early. The gain inverts the first time a reader recognizes the cadence: the familiar rhythm, the same five favorite words. The ease you gained is repaid from your credibility — and credibility discounts everything that comes after.

09 · The choice that isn't

Reject AI

Keep your voice, lose the leverage. Watch the people who didn't reject it move faster than you, forever.

Surrender to it

Keep the leverage, lose the voice. Sound a little more like everyone else every month, and call it productivity.

Both doors are losses. There is a third one.

10 · The third way

Author your own context.
Improve what you do.
Don't replace it.

A plain-text file you wrote, on your device, handed to whatever assistant you already use. Your names, your judgment, your voice — as the ground the machine stands on, instead of the average.

Appendix A · Push back on us

"Homogenization is speculative."

The frontier of it, yes — we cite the studies and say "early." What is not speculative: memory-on-by-default, unauthored profiles, and the before/after delta each participant measures on their own work. The argument never rests its weight on the scary slide.

"This sounds anti-AI. We fund adoption."

The program is four evenings of hands-on building with each person's own assistant. The foundations hour exists so the adoption is informed. We teach use — the warning is the seatbelt, not the car.

"Voice is a luxury; my job seekers need resumes now."

Every applicant now submits AI-polished materials, and screeners pattern-match the house style. For job seekers, sameness is not a luxury problem — it is the rejection reason. Differentiation is the outcome.

Appendix B · And the honest concessions

"Isn't this prompt engineering with extra steps?"

Prompt phrasing doesn't transfer between assistants — the research is clear. An owned file does. That difference is demonstrable in the room, in five minutes.

"You teach people not to sound like Claude… using Claude."

The same way writing class uses a word processor. The tool drafts; the file and the person decide. Improve, not replace — said early, every time.

What we concede without a fight

Average is fine for boilerplate — the file exists for the writing that carries your name. And a context file steers voice; it doesn't guarantee it. The promise is authorship plus evidence, never magic.
