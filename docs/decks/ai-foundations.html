01 · The trick behind the magic

Hope this email finds you  ___ 

You filled that in before I finished the sentence. So does the machine — for every word it writes. The AI your people just adopted is autocomplete at scale: it continues with what most people say.

A simplification an expert accepts: predicting the most likely next word is the literal training objective.

02 · The objective, plainly

It always bets on average.

Not a flaw — the definition. The most likely word is the most common one, so every draft it hands your people starts life as the most common version of the thought.

03 · The other direction

And it reads them the same way.

Interpretation is prediction too: handed your analyst's request, it lands on the most likely meaning — the average asker's meaning. What it misreads is exactly what you pay people for: their judgment, their shorthand, what they know that the average person doesn't. And the misread is silent — a confident answer to a question nobody asked.

Rewording alone swings measured accuracy up to 76 points (Sclar et al., ICLR 2024). Models misread — and covertly penalize — non-standard dialects (Hofmann et al., Nature 2024). It does not read all of your people equally well.

07 · Relying on only their memory

The honest ledger.

What it does well for your people

Zero effort — it fills itself. Continuity across chats. The mundane, reliably: tone, recurring facts, updates nobody would think to record. Concede all of it; it's true.

What it cannot do

Confirm anything with them. Tell anyone when it rewrote itself. Move to another assistant. Read their atypical as anything but noise. Serve your organization's goals over its maker's.

Let them use it — and never rely on only it. Their memory is the model's reading of your people, stored: every misread conditions the next. A memory nobody authored is a lens nobody can focus.

Appendix A · Push back on us

"The homogenization risk is speculative."

The frontier of it, yes — we cite the studies and say "early." Not speculative: memory-on-by-default, unauthored profiles, and the before/after delta your own people measure on their own work. The case never rests its weight on the scary slide.

"This sounds anti-AI. We're trying to drive adoption."

The program is four evenings of hands-on building with each person's own assistant. The foundations hour exists so the adoption is informed. We teach use — the warning is the seatbelt, not the car.

"My people need output now, not voice."

Everyone's output is now AI-polished, and readers pattern-match the house style — in hiring, in sales, in grants. When output is table stakes, the differentiator IS whose voice survives. That's an output argument, not a taste argument.

Appendix B · And the honest concessions

"Isn't this prompt training with extra steps?"

Prompt phrasing doesn't transfer between assistants — the research is clear. An owned file does. Your people demonstrate the difference themselves, in the room, in five minutes.

"You teach people not to sound like Claude… using Claude."

The same way writing class uses a word processor. The tool drafts; the file and the person decide. Improve, not replace — said early, every time.

What we concede without a fight

Average is fine for boilerplate — the file exists for the work that carries a name. And a context file steers voice; it doesn't guarantee it. The promise is authorship plus evidence, never magic.
