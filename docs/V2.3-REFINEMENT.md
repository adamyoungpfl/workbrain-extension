## THE THREE FLAGS

**FLAG 1 · The orientation demo brushes the no-tour guardrail — and passes on the V1.1 rule.**
`docs/GUARDRAILS.md` bans "a tour, a coach-mark overlay". V1.1 already drew the line that saves
this: interstitials are legal as **pure in-flow narrative, never a dismissible overlay pointing at
UI**. VB-90 is built as real interview steps — the same beat machinery, advancing on the same Next
— that happen to *show* the real drawer in both modes while they talk. Nothing floats, nothing
points, nothing is dismissible-without-reading.

**FLAG 2 · "No goal, no go" is a gate, and gates are what VB-34/VB-73 spent two rounds removing.**
Adam's call is explicit and it is his to make — recorded, built. What keeps it inside the
product's ethos: the gate is **two real questions at the top of the flow**, not a lock on the
splash — the person is answering from second one, which is the opposite of a toll booth; their
goal is *used* (it becomes the proof-loop's question and the reflect step's vocabulary), so the
gate is the first useful thing rather than a hurdle; and the examples steer toward workable goals
(FLAG: the "obscure or illicit topics" guardrail Adam asked for is carried by example-shaping and
a soft "your AI may decline some topics" line, never by us judging their text).

**FLAG 3 · Labels that persist "even though unreadable" collide with the contrast floor.**
`docs/GUARDRAILS.md`: text ≥ 4.5:1, and navMelt's rule — never a frame of faint text. VB-77's cut
at the depth floor exists because of it. **Built as the floor-respecting version of the illusion:**
labels never fade below `LABEL_OPACITY_MIN` (8.8:1) and never below 11.2px — they persist at the
far side at full legal strength, small, which reads as distance. The one thing NOT done is
painting sub-floor text. If the far labels still read as "disappearing" in the morning, the
remaining lever is size, not opacity.

### VB-100 · The context-window claim *(spike only — answered below, nothing built)*
**Viable, honestly, as a bounded claim — not as a precise %.** Tokens ≈ chars/4 within ~±15% for
English prose; window sizes are public per model but change often and vary by tier. So the honest
metric is a RANGE against a NAMED window: *"Your file: ~1,900 tokens — under 1% of Claude's
200k window. The other 99% stays free for today's work."* What must never ship: a precise
percent, a claim about "your account's" window (unknowable), or any live tokenizer dependency.
The value framing that fits Adam's ask — people who care about making tokens useful, not saving
them — is **focus**: "you're not spending context, you're aiming it." Recommend: one line on the
file view + download toast, per-service numbers in a small table maintained beside
`PROOF_SERVICES`, reviewed each release. Build in a later round, on this basis.
