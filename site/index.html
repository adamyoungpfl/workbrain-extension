Why it goes stale

Different answers rot at different speeds. A job title lasts years; this quarter's goal lasts a quarter. Every answer carries the date you gave it and a half-life, so the panel can tell you which parts are still true instead of asking you to re-read the whole file.

Current — answered recently enough to trust
Drifting — past its half-life, worth a look
Unanswered — never filled in, and that is fine

White paper · v1.0

The design of a portable context file.

On the shape of the file, the schema behind it, the freshness model that keeps it honest, and the guardrails the whole design is held to.

Abstract

Assistants are increasingly good at the work and still bad at the person. The gap is not capability, it is context: the answer arrives fluent and generic because nothing in the prompt says who is asking, what they are responsible for, or what a good answer looks like to them.

This paper describes a file — plain markdown, generated from a structured interview, held entirely on the person's own machine — designed to close that gap without an account, a server, or a dependency on any one assistant. It sets out the section schema and why the boundaries fall where they do, the round-trip guarantee that makes generate-and-parse lossless, and a freshness model in which every answer carries a timestamp and a half-life so the file can report which of its own claims are still likely true.

It then examines evaluation: how a file can be tested against a baseline captured before it existed, how a skill can be scored against the objective its author stated, why a model's self-report about its own use of context is a claim rather than a trace, and what that means for tuning. It closes with the guardrails — never transmit the person's content, never notify, never hide a prompt — and the one written amendment under which a hosted testing environment is permitted to exist.

Privacy & guardrails

The promise, and its one amendment.

Never transmit the person's content anywhere. Not to us, not to their AI without them seeing the exact text first, not to a pack publisher.

The guardrail the product is built on, unabridged.

What stays

Every answer you give, in your browser's own storage Both files, generated locally, downloaded by you Your captured baseline, and every proof run's history

What can leave, and only if you press it

A proof run: your file or skill plus the task, to a named model, discarded on response Feedback you choose to write and send Nothing else. Ever, and not quietly.

The amendment: hosted proving

A hosted test environment can transmit the file, which the guardrail above forbids. Rather than make that a quiet exception somebody finds in a network tab, it is written down here.

What is sent
The prompt — your file or a single skill, plus a task. No identifiers, no session, no answer store.
Where it goes
Through us to a named model, and discarded on response.
Is it a default
No. Copy-and-paste stays, and stays first. This is a door.
Hidden prompts
None. If extra instructions exist, you read them before they send.

One thing we will not claim: that a model can report your environment, location or device. It sees the prompt. Anything else it says about your machine is invention, and tuning a file on invention is worse than not tuning it.
